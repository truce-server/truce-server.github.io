In other words, this song was the end of an era that lasted for over a decade. He also wrote this song way back in 2012, releasing it over a decade later, always intending it to be the Grand Finale of Subtract but waiting for the right time to release the album. And because he held on to it so much, to me it really does encompass the whole era coming full circle. The song ties very heavily into the folk genre and stripped down rawness which describes a lot of his early work, but with a very clean and harmonious production and lyrical depth reflecting his later albums. It's got a dramatic beginning, clear vocals, amazing harmonies, and a really comforting yet bittersweet feeling. And that's really why I think this is magnum opus. It's wrapping up a journey, reflecting on all the different stages of it, celebrating his evolution as an artist. It's not just a pop song that ticks all the boxes, it's a song that feels very true to who Ed Sheeran is, and his story, from the beginning to where he is now. The song itself contrasts the haunting beauty of the Scottish Highlands with the nostalgic longing for a loved one that's been torn away from him, and wanting to reunite and fall in love in the Hills of Aberfeldy. It's sad, it's chilling, and idk it hits hard because while this song can most reasonably be taken romantically, having a loved one ripped from your life in any context is devastating, yet when you think of them you think of all the beautiful things it brought you and it's comforting in some way, and I think this song really reflects that longing feeling really well.
