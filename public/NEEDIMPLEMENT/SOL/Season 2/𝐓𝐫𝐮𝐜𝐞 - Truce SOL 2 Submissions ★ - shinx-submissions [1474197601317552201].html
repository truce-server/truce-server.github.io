ROUND 1: I Want You To Love Me - Fiona Apple

Fetch the Bolt Cutters (2020)

THE ALBUM

Fiona Apple's latest full length, Fetch the Bolt Cutters, is a unique entry in her discography in the sense that it definitively is her most DIY album yet, with most of the songs being produced and recorded in Apple's home studio using Garageband. This gave the album a more unpolished and clearly improvised feel, yet thrives off its unconventionality and Apple's stellar-as-ever songwriting.
12:01 PM

THE SONG

Yes, those are dolphin noises at the end of the song, its all part of her genius, don't worry. Anyways, I Want You to Love Me provides a very mature and introspective look into the desire to embrace love and be loved; it was originally conceptualised for a hypothetical lover, but was later written around Fiona Apple's experiences dating writer Jonathan Ames and revelations she had while meditating. The song can be neatly broken down into three phases: the first phase acknowledges the good, the bad and everything between that has led Apple to her current stage in life; in the chorus, she sings about her earnest hopes that she's loved by the person she's singing to. The next phase explores Apple's philosophical views on how her time on the world will inevitably lead to a largely meaningless death in the grand scale of the world. Despite this, she won't let this viewpoint stop her humane desire to want love in her life, which in the scale of a simple human life, means more than life itself.
And I know none of this will matter in the long run But I know a sound is still a sound around no one And while I'm in this body I want somebody to want And I want what I want and I want
The final phase then dwindles a bit, as Apple recognises that the person she wants does reciprocate her feelings, albeit they're more reluctant to acknowledge it. She promises her lover that she's even with past transgressions, she's willing to begin anew wherever they want to set afoot, and that she's willing to wait until the moment where they finally embrace and love her. My personal recommendation? Read the lyrics for Fiona Apple's music. She's so fucking good with lyrics I swear to god

ROUND 9: The Air Up Here - Origami Angel

Somewhere City (2019)

THE ALBUM

Origami Angel's debut album, Somewhere City is a loose concept album depicting the titular setting, a place coined by lead vocalist Ryland Heagy as 'a place he likes to go when he feels sad'. And true to its nature, it's perhaps one of the most optimistic and upbeat emo albums to ever be penned, and a landmark album in modern emo culture.

THE SONG

Please hear me out on this. The Air Up Here serves as the album's closer, not only there to create a perfect loop that syncs with the opening notes of opener Welcome To..., but also to serve as the ultimate encapsulation of Somewhere City's overall themes of finding happiness within oneself. And I personally see that there's a deeper meaning to the album: see, its a picturesque depiction of how we can find ourselves loving the world by first loving ourselves, because the most optimistic version of ourselves is always capable of seeing the bright side of the things around us, therefore allowing us to truly embrace the world as one we love.
I'm never gonna come back home again I'm never gonna feel that low again It's nothing that you ever did wrong It's just I found a place to call home I'm finally somewhere that I have to be Where I’ve been living peacefully and happily To paradise out of a catastrophe And I feel worthwhile now ’Cause the city never lets me down
Essentially, through Somewhere City, we're able to find a world within ourselves that we can truly appreciate, thereby allowing someone to:
Just know that you’ll never be alone There's places you can go, there's people you should know You don't have to do anything or be anyone, just follow the road
And if that isn't enough to convince you, maybe the pleasant memories that come with Somewhere City's progression should sweeten the deal?
